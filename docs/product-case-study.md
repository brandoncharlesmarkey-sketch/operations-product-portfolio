# Case Study: From Fragmented Operations to Actionable Decisions

## Problem

A multi-site healthcare support organization depended on separate spreadsheets and manual processes to manage scheduling, dispatch, transportation, staffing exceptions, vehicle availability, and performance reporting. That fragmentation made it harder to understand demand, make timely labor decisions, and provide consistent service.

## My role

I was the operations leader and hands-on product owner. I defined workflows with frontline users and managers, designed system logic, built and iterated on tools with AI-assisted development, and used operating data to guide deployment and refinement.

## Approach

1. Mapped the everyday decisions dispatchers, supervisors, and managers needed to make.
2. Consolidated operational signals such as staffing coverage, calls, vehicle status, and historical demand.
3. Developed a unified operations interface, reporting workflows, and alerts for exceptions and projected overtime.
4. Used AI-assisted analysis of employee transportation demand, routing patterns, and staffing usage to identify efficiency opportunities.
5. Worked with the client to redesign shuttle routing and service levels without materially compromising availability.

## Reported operating outcomes

- **5% improvement in overtime performance**.
- **15% improvement in shuttle response times**.
- **Two vehicles removed** from the operating model with minimal service impact, reducing fuel, maintenance, and fleet costs.
- Staffing against budget became markedly more consistent, moving away from repeated reactive adjustments.

Results are drawn from operational leadership experience and are presented without customer-sensitive underlying records. The metrics reflect the scope described above and should not be interpreted as a controlled experiment.

## Product and management lessons

Technology only creates value when it changes an operational decision. Good workflows need timely, accurate data, clear ownership of exceptions, and controls that let supervisors act without losing visibility into staffing, service quality, or costs.
